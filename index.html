About me

Plant adaptation at the intersection of genomes, microbes, and the environment

My research seeks to understand how plants are shaped by the environments they experience and by the microorganisms they interact with.

I combine comparative genomics, transcriptomics, microbiome analysis, phylogenetics, and systems biology to investigate how genes, microbial communities, and their interactions contribute to plant adaptation and stress responses.

I work across biological scales, from genes and microbial communities to whole-plant responses, with a particular interest in grasses and crop species. Ultimately, my goal is to identify biological mechanisms and molecular signatures that can help improve crop and soil resilience, productivity, and performance under changing environmental conditions.
