Open science & code

GitHub & Bioinformatics

Reproducible workflows and computational resources for genomics, microbial ecology, molecular biology, and multi-omics research.

I develop computational workflows as part of my research and make code, documentation, and analytical resources publicly available whenever possible. These projects range from long-read microbiome analysis to comparative genomics, multi-omics integration, metatranscriptomics, and bacterial genome reconstruction.

Visit my GitHub

01 — Long-read Microbiome Workflow

Reproducible analysis of full-length microbial amplicon sequencing data.

A computational workflow for processing full-length Oxford Nanopore 16S and ITS sequencing data, from sequence quality control and organelle removal to taxonomic classification and downstream ecological analysis.

Oxford Nanopore 16S ITS Emu HPC Microbial ecology

02 — Pan-Grass Comparative Genomics

Large-scale comparative genomics across grass species.

Computational approaches for integrating genome annotation, orthology, synteny, and evolutionary information across grass genomes to investigate gene conservation, diversification, and functional evolution.

Comparative genomics Synteny Poaceae Gene evolution Public databases
Repository coming soon Associated publication

03 — Multicopy Gene Analysis

Computational and experimental strategies for resolving highly similar gene copies.

A workflow combining genomic searches, sequence comparison, public RNA-seq data, and experimental validation to investigate multicopy genes and distinguish paralogue-specific expression and biological function.

Gene families Paralogues RNA-seq Expression analysis Experimental validation
Repository coming soon Associated publication

04 — Gene & Pathway Evolution Analysis

Integrative reconstruction of gene and metabolic pathway evolution.

A bioinformatics framework for investigating the evolutionary history of genes and metabolic pathways by integrating sequence similarity, phylogenetics, functional annotation, domain architecture, and comparative genomic information.

Phylogenetics Gene evolution Pathway evolution Functional annotation Comparative genomics
View repository Associated publication

05 — Multi-omics & Molecular Ecology Workflows

Integrating multiple molecular layers to connect community structure with biological activity and ecosystem function.

Developing analytical workflows for metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, amplicon sequencing, and other molecular datasets with an emphasis on reproducibility, biological interpretation, and integration across multiple omics layers.

Metagenomics Metatranscriptomics Multi-omics Molecular ecology Data integration Reproducible bioinformatics
Repository coming soon

06 — Bacterial Long-Read Genome Assembly, Annotation & Curation

Reproducible workflows for bacterial genome reconstruction from long-read sequencing data.

A computational workflow for bacterial isolate genome analysis using long-read sequencing, including read quality control, genome assembly, polishing, genome quality assessment, annotation, and manual curation. The goal is to generate accurate and biologically interpretable genome assemblies suitable for comparative genomics and downstream functional analysis.

Oxford Nanopore Genome assembly Polishing Genome annotation Genome curation Comparative genomics
Repository coming soon

07 — Soil Metatranscriptome Pipeline

Reproducible processing and functional analysis of soil metatranscriptomic data.

A computational workflow for soil metatranscriptome analysis, including read quality control, removal of non-informative sequences, transcript quantification, functional annotation, differential expression, and pathway-level interpretation. The pipeline is designed to connect transcriptional activity with microbial community function and biogeochemical processes.

Metatranscriptomics RNA-seq Soil microbiome Functional annotation Differential expression Pathway analysis HPC

Open and reproducible research

My goal is not only to produce analytical code for individual projects, but to develop workflows that can be documented, reused, tested, and adapted to new biological questions. Repositories will continue to be expanded as pipelines and associated datasets are prepared for public release.
