Research program

Connecting plants, microbes, and environmental change across biological scales

My research investigates how plants, microbial communities, and the environment interact across biological scales.

I combine evolutionary genomics, experimental biology, microbial ecology, multi-omics, and computational approaches to understand how environmental conditions shape biological responses — from genes and microbial activity to plant performance and ecosystem processes.

Across my research, computational tools support biological discovery rather than define the questions themselves. My goal is to connect molecular mechanisms with ecological function and ultimately identify biological processes that contribute to plant and soil resilience under environmental change.

01 — Plant–Microbe Interactions

Scientific question

How do host identity, environmental history, and microbial partners interact to shape plant adaptation and stress responses?

Plants experience environmental stress while simultaneously recruiting, filtering, and responding to microbial partners. I investigate how host identity and environmental history influence these interactions and whether microbial functions contribute consistently to plant performance across environmental contexts.

My work has moved from molecular interactions between sugarcane and its associated microorganisms to complex plant-associated microbial communities. This includes transcriptional responses during sugarcane–pathogen interactions, genotype-associated variation in yellow pea nodule microbiomes, recovery of microbiome information from previously unmapped sugarcane transcriptomic reads, and microbial assembly associated with native grasses.

Current focus

Environmental history and native grass–microbiome interactions
DRYiNG project · long-term drought · native grasslands · plant recovery

Within the DRYiNG experiment, I am investigating how long-term drought history influences belowground microbial states and how these states may relate to the recovery of native northern grasslands following drought.

Future direction

I aim to determine when environmentally conditioned microbiomes influence plant stress tolerance, how strongly these responses depend on host identity, and whether selected microbial functions can operate across native plants and crop species.

02 — Soil Microbial Ecology & Biogeochemistry

Scientific question

How do environmental history and resource availability regulate microbial activity, carbon allocation, nutrient cycling, and soil function?

Microbial communities regulate major carbon and nutrient transformations in soil, yet changes in community composition do not necessarily predict changes in microbial activity. I investigate how environmental history and resource availability reorganize microbial metabolism and when these functional changes translate into altered biogeochemical processes.

My current carbon-pulse work examines this distinction directly by integrating microbial community profiles, metatranscriptomics, and respiration. The observation that transcriptional responses can occur without corresponding taxonomic turnover has motivated a broader interest in the decoupling among microbial composition, realized activity, and ecosystem function.

Current focus

Microbial responses to carbon availability
Carbon pulse · community structure · transcription · nutrient cycling

I am studying how short- and long-term carbon availability reorganizes microbial transcription and metabolism relative to changes in community structure, and how those responses relate to carbon and nutrient cycling.

Developing direction

Microbial drought memory, resuscitation, and carbon allocation
DRYiNG × JGI framework · drought legacy · rewetting · carbon use efficiency

My developing work asks whether long-term drought leaves microorganisms in a physiological state that changes how they resuscitate when water returns. The DRYiNG–JGI framework integrates existing metagenomes and MAGs with time-resolved metatranscriptomics, metabolomics, respiration, and H₂¹⁸O-based microbial growth measurements to test whether pre-rewetting molecular states can predict subsequent growth, respiration, and carbon use efficiency.

03 — Multi-omics & Bioinformatics

Scientific question

How can reproducible computational approaches and existing molecular datasets be integrated to extract new biological information from complex systems?

Biological datasets increasingly contain information beyond the question for which they were originally generated. My computational research focuses on developing reproducible analytical workflows while also reusing genomic, transcriptomic, and microbiome resources to address new biological questions.

This work spans comparative genomics, gene-family evolution, transcriptomics, long-read microbiome analysis, and multi-omics integration. Rather than treating bioinformatics as an endpoint, I use computational methods to generate experimentally testable hypotheses and connect molecular patterns with biological function.

Current directions

Comparative genomics and public-data reuse
Grass genomes · synteny · gene-family evolution · evolutionary genomics

I use large comparative genomic datasets to explore how evolutionary history shapes gene conservation, diversification, and environmental response across plant lineages.

Reproducible molecular ecology pipelines
Nanopore · short reads · HPC · microbiome analysis

I develop reproducible workflows for processing and interpreting short- and long-read molecular ecology datasets, with an emphasis on accuracy, transparency, and reusability.

Integrating public omics with experimental validation
RNA-seq · gene families · expression analysis · multi-omics

I am interested in combining public molecular datasets with targeted experimental validation to investigate biological questions that extend beyond the original purpose of the datasets.

Future direction

I aim to develop efficient and accurate molecular ecology frameworks that integrate short- and long-read sequencing, public molecular databases, and multiple omics layers, while creating reusable pipelines that can support new ecological and evolutionary questions.

Across the program

Across these research directions, I develop new bioinformatics pipelines, establish experimental plant and soil systems, generate integrated omics datasets, and use emerging results to formulate new biological questions. The common goal is to move from descriptive patterns toward mechanisms that connect genes, organisms, microbial communities, and ecosystem function.
